About me

How I got here

When I was ten, Google seemed to have an answer for every question I could think of, including the ones my teachers waved off as distractions. I spent a long time wondering how that could possibly work. Then a teacher explained that computers run on their own language of ones and zeros, and it clicked: if I learned to speak it, I could build tools like that too.

I studied chemical engineering at IIT Patna, partly because it left room for interdisciplinary work later. That room got used. In the Process Systems Engineering Lab I trained SVM and Random Forest classifiers to sort feasible steel production plans from infeasible ones, and the work became my first published paper. Along the way I spent a summer as a data science intern at Henry Harvin, building sentiment classifiers and dashboards.

After graduating in 2023 I joined Carelon in Hyderabad as an Associate Software Engineer. For two years I worked on GenAI for health insurance: a RAG system that answers questions from policy documents, a claims pipeline that shows reviewers where every extracted value came from, and a set of agents that handle member requests like address changes and ID cards. Carelon management gave me the Impact Award three times for that work.

In September 2025 I started an MS in Computer Science at Northeastern University. Lately I've been working closer to the models themselves: aligning smaller language models with knowledge distillation and LoRA, and implementing reinforcement learning algorithms to compare how they behave.

What I care about

I think carefully designed AI can do a lot of good in public health. The problems that pull me in most are healthcare tools that work for underserved communities, and systems that help public health teams predict, prevent, and respond to crises.

Carelon shaped how I think about doing that responsibly. When a model reads a claim or answers a policy question, a person should be able to see where the answer came from. So I care about grounding outputs in their sources, reporting precision and recall rather than a single accuracy number, and keeping a human in the loop when the stakes are high.

What's next

I graduate in May 2027 and I'm looking for data science and AI roles. If you're working on retrieval, language models, or applied machine learning and want to talk, email me at sharthiabhinay@gmail.com or find me on LinkedIn.
